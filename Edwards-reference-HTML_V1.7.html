17 Long-Term Outcomes of the RESILIA Tissue Valve: COMMENCE Aortic Trial
Infographic ▼

Infographic

What was covered
  • Advances such as RESILIA tissue technology reduce calcification and structural valve deterioration (SVD).
  • COMMENCE is an FDA trial across 27 sites in the US, Canada and Europe. Extended follow-up (years 6 to 10) covers 225 re-consented patients at 10 sites, mean age 65.1 and EuroSCORE II 2.1.

Aortic stenosis (AS)

Phase I, II, III

The aortic valve leaflets thicken, calcify, and stiffen over time — most often degenerative calcific disease in older adults, or accelerated by an underlying bicuspid valve in younger patients. As the orifice narrows, the left ventricle has to generate much higher pressure to push blood through, so the ventricular wall thickens in response (concentric hypertrophy). Left untreated, this pressure overload eventually outpaces the heart's ability to compensate.

Classic symptoms follow a well-known triad — chest pain, fainting or near-fainting (syncope), and breathlessness — and once symptoms appear, prognosis worsens sharply without intervention. Severity is graded primarily by aortic valve area, mean pressure gradient, and jet velocity on echocardiography.

Mechanism
Leaflet calcification narrows the orifice
Ventricular effect
Pressure overload, concentric hypertrophy
Classic symptoms
Angina, syncope, dyspnea
Graded by
Valve area, gradient, jet velocity

Edwards therapies for AS

SAPIEN 3 / SAPIEN 3 Ultra RESILIA Transcatheter — TAVR
▼

Edwards' balloon-expandable transcatheter heart valve, delivered by catheter without open-heart surgery. It is the most extensively studied valve in TAVR, with the deepest base of randomized long-term evidence in the field.

  • SAPIEN 3 Ultra adds a taller outer skirt over the base SAPIEN 3 design to further reduce paravalvular leak
  • RESILIA tissue treatment is applied to reduce calcification and extend durability
  • Approved across the surgical risk spectrum — inoperable through low-risk patients
Clinical evidence — TAVR
  • PARTNER 1 — the original landmark trial establishing TAVR as an alternative to surgery in high-risk and inoperable patients with severe AS.

Aortic regurgitation (AR)

Phase I, II, III

Here the mechanical problem is the opposite of stenosis: the valve fails to close fully, so blood leaks backward from the aorta into the left ventricle during diastole. Causes include a bicuspid valve, dilation of the aortic root, degenerative leaflet disease, or endocarditis. Because the ventricle is now handling a volume overload rather than a pressure overload, it responds by dilating (eccentric hypertrophy) rather than thickening.

AR can be chronic and silently compensated for years before decompensating, or acute and poorly tolerated (e.g. after endocarditis or aortic dissection). A key structural difference from AS: the leaflets are usually not calcified, which removes the anchoring surface that transcatheter aortic valves rely on — this is the central engineering challenge behind treating AR with catheter-based devices.

Mechanism
Incomplete leaflet closure, diastolic leak
Ventricular effect
Volume overload, eccentric dilation
Course
Often chronic/compensated; can be acute
Key challenge
Usually non-calcified — hard to anchor a valve

Edwards therapies for AR

INSPIRIS RESILIA aortic valve Surgical — SAVR
▼

The same surgical bioprosthesis used in AS is the mainstay for AR as well — the valve is indicated for replacement of native or prosthetic aortic valves generally, not for a specific disease mechanism. Surgery remains the standard of care for AR because it doesn't depend on calcium for anchoring the way a transcatheter valve does.

Clinical evidence — SAVR (RESILIA platform)
  • COMMENCE — pivotal IDE trial for RESILIA tissue in the aortic position, underpinning durability claims across aortic valve disease including AR. Valve durability
  • INDURE Registry — long-term durability registry in patients under 60, relevant to younger AR patients who need decades of valve life.
  • 8-year propensity-matched analysis — real-world durability comparison at extended follow-up.
KONECT RESILIA aortic valved conduit Surgical — root replacement
▼

A combined valve-and-conduit graft used when AR is accompanied by disease of the aortic root or ascending aorta (for example annuloaortic ectasia) — the kind of case that needs a Bentall-style composite root replacement rather than an isolated valve swap.

  • Pairs the RESILIA-treated tissue valve with a Valsalva-design vascular conduit in a single, pre-assembled device
  • Addresses both the incompetent valve and the diseased root in one procedure, reducing surgical steps versus a self-assembled conduit

Shares its RESILIA tissue evidence base with INSPIRIS RESILIA (COMMENCE and related RESILIA durability analyses); it is a newer platform with its own real-world registry (KONECTION) now recruiting.

Transcatheter option No dedicated Edwards device
▼

Edwards does not currently have a transcatheter valve specifically indicated for pure native AR. SAPIEN 3/Ultra is labeled for calcific AS, and its balloon-expandable frame relies on calcium contact for anchoring — implanting it off-label in non-calcified AR anatomy carries a materially higher risk of valve migration or embolization.

Purpose-built "dedicated" AR devices with leaflet-clipping anchoring mechanisms exist from other manufacturers and have recently picked up a conditional guideline recommendation — worth knowing about for competitive/landscape context, but they are not part of the Edwards portfolio.

Note for content accuracy: any AR asset referencing a "dedicated transcatheter AR valve" as an Edwards product should be flagged — that class of device is not currently Edwards-branded.

Mitral regurgitation (MR)

Phase III

The mitral valve sits between the left atrium and left ventricle. When it fails to close properly, blood leaks backward into the atrium during systole. MR splits into two mechanistically distinct categories that drive different treatment choices:

Primary (degenerative) MR is a problem with the valve apparatus itself — leaflet prolapse, a flail leaflet, or ruptured chordae. Secondary (functional) MR occurs when the valve is structurally normal but a dilated, poorly contracting left ventricle pulls the leaflets out of their normal coaptation position.

Either way, the leaking blood volume overloads the left atrium and pulmonary circulation, driving symptoms of congestion, atrial fibrillation, and heart failure. Severity is graded by regurgitant volume, regurgitant fraction, and effective regurgitant orifice area (EROA).

Mechanism
Incompetent mitral valve, systolic backflow
Two types
Primary (leaflet) vs secondary (ventricular)
Downstream effect
Atrial/pulmonary volume overload
Graded by
Regurgitant volume, fraction, EROA

Edwards therapies for MR

PASCAL Precision system Transcatheter — TEER
▼

Edwards' transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) system. Delivered transfemorally and transseptally, it uses clip-and-pad elements to grasp and approximate the mitral leaflets, reducing the regurgitant gap without removing the native valve.

  • FDA-approved for degenerative (primary) MR in patients at prohibitive surgical risk; also CE marked for both mitral and tricuspid regurgitation
  • Real-world registry data (STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy Registry) covering thousands of PASCAL patients support its sustained safety and effectiveness
  • A repair option, not a replacement — preserves the native valve architecture
SAPIEN M3 system Transcatheter — TMVR
▼

Built on the proven SAPIEN valve platform, SAPIEN M3 was the first transcatheter mitral valve replacement system approved via a fully transseptal, transfemoral approach — no surgical access point needed.

  • Two-step delivery: an anchoring "dock" is placed first, then the valve itself
  • Indicated for patients unsuitable for surgery or TEER, including those with mitral annular calcification (MAC) where repair devices struggle to get a durable result
  • Supported by the ENCIRCLE pivotal trial data; a replacement option used when the valve anatomy isn't a good candidate for repair
MITRIS RESILIA mitral valve Surgical — replacement
▼

The surgical bioprosthetic option for mitral valve replacement, carrying the same RESILIA anti-calcification tissue treatment as the INSPIRIS aortic valve, adapted to the mitral position with a saddle-shaped sewing cuff designed to mimic the native annulus. Surgery — repair where feasible, replacement where not — remains the guideline-preferred route for lower-risk, younger patients.

Clinical evidence — SAVR (RESILIA platform)
  • COMMENCE — includes a dedicated mitral cohort; the pivotal evidence base for MITRIS RESILIA. Valve durability
  • 5-year COMMENCE follow-up — the mitral cohort's 5-year event-free rates were 79.9% for all-cause mortality, 98.7% for freedom from structural valve deterioration, and 97.1% for freedom from reoperation.
  • 8-year RESILIA analyses — pooled longer-term RESILIA tissue performance data spanning both aortic and mitral cohorts. Valve durability
